Key Concepts ▾
Why learning rate matters: The learning rate controls how big a step the optimizer takes each update. Too high and training diverges — the loss explodes. Too low and training is painfully slow and may get stuck in a sharp local minimum. The right LR is the single most impactful hyperparameter.
LR too high vs too low: A high LR causes the optimizer to overshoot the minimum — loss oscillates or diverges. A low LR makes the gradient steps tiny — training converges but takes far more epochs and may settle in a flat, suboptimal region. The sweet spot is problem-dependent.
What warmup does: At initialization, weights are random and gradients are noisy and large. Starting with a very small LR and ramping up prevents catastrophic early updates from damaging the model before it has learned anything useful. Warmup is especially important for Transformers.
Why cosine annealing works well: Cosine schedules smoothly reduce the LR following a half-cosine curve — fast initial progress when far from the minimum, gentle fine-tuning near convergence. The smooth decay avoids the abrupt jumps of step decay and often finds flatter, more generalizable minima.
What cyclical LR captures: Cyclical learning rates periodically increase the LR after decreasing it. This "annealing with restarts" helps escape sharp local minima and saddle points — the optimizer can explore more of the loss landscape and often settles in a wider, flatter minimum with better generalization.
