Key Concepts ▾
What is bias? Bias is the systematic error from a model that is too simple to capture the true pattern — a linear model fit to curved data will always miss the curve, no matter how much training data you give it. High bias → underfitting.
What is variance? Variance is how much a model's predictions fluctuate when trained on different samples from the same distribution. A degree-9 polynomial memorizes its training set but fits a completely different curve for each new sample. High variance → overfitting.
The bias-variance tradeoff: Total prediction error = Bias² + Variance + Noise. Reducing bias (more complex model) increases variance; reducing variance (simpler model) increases bias. Optimal complexity minimizes their sum — this is the "sweet spot."
Underfitting: The model is too simple — it has high bias and low variance. Both training and test error are high. The model predicts roughly the same wrong answer regardless of which training samples it sees.
Overfitting: The model is too complex — it has low bias but high variance. Training error is very low but test error is much higher. The model memorizes noise in the training data and fails to generalize.
How to fix it: Regularization (L2/L1) penalizes large coefficients, reducing variance at the cost of slight bias increase. More training data reduces variance without touching bias. Early stopping and dropout apply the same principle in neural networks.
