Key Concepts ▾
Vanishing gradients: sigmoid and tanh derivatives are at most ~0.25 and 1.0, and are often much smaller. Multiplied together across many layers, even small derivatives compound to near-zero — early layers receive almost no gradient signal.
Exploding gradients: if weight matrices have norms greater than 1, backpropagated gradients can compound multiplicatively and grow without bound, causing unstable updates and NaN loss.
Chain rule: backpropagation multiplies gradients layer-by-layer: ∂L/∂W₁ = (∂L/∂aₙ) × (∂aₙ/∂aₙ₋₁) × … × (∂a₂/∂a₁). Each factor is a derivative of the activation — the product of many factors <1 collapses to zero.
Depth problem: in a 10-layer sigmoid network, the gradient reaching layer 1 is (0.25)¹⁰ ≈ 0.000001 — a million-fold reduction. Early layers barely update, effectively freezing them during training.
Modern fixes: ReLU removes the saturation problem (derivative = 1 for positive values). Skip connections (ResNets) create gradient highways. Xavier/He initialization keeps activation variance stable. Gradient clipping caps runaway gradients.

Same deep network — four different gradient stability fixes
Batch Normalization
Normalizes layer inputs to zero mean and unit variance — keeps activation magnitudes from drifting, stabilizing gradients throughout training.
Normalization
Skip Connections (ResNet)
Identity shortcuts let gradients bypass layers entirely — the gradient highway ensures every layer receives at least a clean copy of the upstream gradient.
Architecture
Xavier / He Initialization
Scales initial weights by 1/√n (Xavier) or √(2/n) (He/ReLU) to keep activation variance stable across layers — prevents vanishing or exploding at the start.
Initialization
Gradient Clipping
Caps gradient norm at a threshold (e.g. 1.0) before the optimizer step — prevents individual parameter updates from becoming catastrophically large.
